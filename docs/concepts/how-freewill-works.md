# How Freewill works

Freewill separates **deciding what to attempt** from **making it happen in a physical world**.

## One decision cycle

1. A villager observes only the world it currently knows: body state, needs, inventory, nearby or remembered resources, relationships, memories, commitments, and social context.
2. The simulation creates legal, fully parameterized actions. An action already names its target, location, recipient, project, or item.
3. The selected decision provider scores those opaque candidate IDs and chooses one.
4. The simulation validates the choice against current state.
5. The engine reserves targets and resources, plans a route, spends time, performs work, and applies consequences exactly once.
6. Events and memories record what occurred. The villager later observes those consequences.

The provider cannot teleport, create an item, invent a target, alter another villager, or complete a project by returning prose.

## Responsive minds, durable actions

The authoritative clock advances in 30-minute steps. That is a decision opportunity, not a requirement to abandon the current plan. Long actions retain commitments and have review boundaries. Critical hunger, thirst, exposure, injury, incapacity, invalid targets, or vanished projects can interrupt immediately.

The renderer interpolates movement independently, which keeps walking smooth without asking a model to choose every footstep.

## No required civilization

Generated worlds start without jobs, public property, currency, government, formal groups, or a common goal. Society systems exist as possible tools. They become available only after the required people, relationships, resources, and decisions exist.

Freewill does not score a brain for producing capitalism, democracy, a bakery, or any other predetermined society. It measures survival and general behavioral consequences, then checks improvements on held-out worlds.
